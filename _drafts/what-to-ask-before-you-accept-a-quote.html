What to ask a bricklayer before you accept their quote

Sharp Bricklaying September 2026 4 min read

The cheapest quote and the best quote are rarely the same one. Two brickies can price the same job and land thousands apart, and the gap usually isn't the bricks. It's what's included in the price, and who's actually on the tools laying them. Here's what's worth asking before you sign anything.

Is everyone quoting the same job?

Before you compare numbers, make sure they're comparing like for like. Ask each brickie what their price covers: scaffold, materials, cartage, cleaning down, waste removal, capping. One quote might look dearer because it's the only one that isn't quietly leaving those costs for you to find later. Get the inclusions in writing so there are no surprises at invoice time.

Who's actually on the tools?

The person who quotes isn't always the person who lays. Ask who'll be on your job day to day, and whether it's the same crew from start to finish. A consistent team keeps the standard even across the whole build. It also means the bloke sorting a query on Thursday is the one who set it out on Monday.

Can you see recent work?

Photos are easy. Ask to see a job they finished in the last few months, and ideally one like yours. If it's a feature wall you're after, ask for a feature wall. Look at the joints, the gauge, and the way they've handled corners and openings. Good brickwork reads clean and consistent right up close, down to the perp joints.

When can they start, and how long will it take?

Good brickies are usually booked ahead. That's a healthy sign, but it has to line up with your build program. Ask when they can start, roughly how long your stage will take, and how they'll keep you posted if the weather or the trade before them shifts the dates. A brickie who plans a couple of months out is one who takes the schedule seriously.

What about the parts you don't see?

Half of good bricklaying is the work that gets covered up. Ask how they handle set-out, damp course, weep holes, wall ties and lintels. Ask how they leave the site for the trades that follow them. That unseen prep is what a following trade either thanks you for or curses. It rarely shows up on a quote, so it's worth raising in the conversation.

How do they leave it?

A wall sitting in a mess of offcuts and mortar splatter isn't a finished wall. Ask what their clean-down and site tidy looks like at handover. It tells you how they think about the whole job, well beyond the laying.

The short version

None of this is about catching anyone out. A good brickie will happily walk you through all of it, because they've already thought it through. When the answers are clear and the work backs them up, price becomes the easy part of the decision.
